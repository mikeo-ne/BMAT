# East Sound: partner alignment and pilot brief

Prepared from the attached **East-Sound-Guide-Users-and-Partners.pdf** (14 pages), repository review and the cited public sources. This is a proposal and product assessment, not legal advice, an audit, an institutional endorsement or a statement that a partnership exists. The guide calls itself a November 2026 guide and cites a 12 November snapshot; treat its date-specific figures as historical claims to verify, not present-day guarantees.

## The answer for URSB

URSB's [copyright directorate](https://ursb.go.ug/2025/03/19/its-all-systems-go-for-the-directorate-of-copyright-and-neighbouring-rights/) describes facilitating registration, supervising CMOs, enforcement and awareness. Its [2025/26–2029/30 strategic plan](https://ursb.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/URSB-Strategic-Plan-IV-2025-2030.pdf) includes improving access and quality of registration services and strengthening communication and awareness. URSB says copyright protection arises automatically for qualifying works, and explains its register and CMO role in its [copyright guidance](https://ursb.go.ug/copyrights/).

**Position East Sound as a supplementary evidence and education tool**: help creators prepare identifiable catalogue information and understand where to register; produce scoped, reviewable records of detected radio uses, with station/time and identifiers where available; help an agreed pilot compare uses with station records and understand missing data. This potentially supports transparency and an evidence-led conversation around CMO supervision. URSB, not East Sound, registers copyright, regulates CMOs or determines compliance. East Sound cannot prove equitable distribution merely from play counts: licensing scope, works-versus-recordings, rights, tariffs, allocation rules and payment data also matter. An ISRC or match is not proof of ownership. Ask URSB for the actual regulatory questions, applicable standards, confidentiality requirements and permitted uses before designing a pilot; do not ask for recognition as if earned.

**Plain-language pitch:** “URSB is working to improve copyright administration and awareness. East Sound could add a limited, independently reviewable picture of music detected on consenting radio stations. We would like your guidance on what evidence, governance and safeguards would make a pilot useful alongside your existing systems.”

## What the repository actually offers / where it needs work

- **Implemented, conditional on configuration and real data:** artist/label catalogue and credit entry; authenticated broadcaster station requests with CMO approval; broadcaster detected-music timeline and PDF/XLSX cue-sheet exports; CMO dashboard, dispute views and monthly PDF/CSV summary; bearer-token JSON/CSV feed constrained by existing RLS (`docs/cmo-data-feed.md`, `docs/station-onboarding.md`). Manual review and actual permitted access remain important. Screens shown in the guide are sample data.
- **Pilot readiness, not proof of performance:** the stream worker and recognition pipeline exist, but end-to-end performance against authorised, independently logged real broadcasts remains unverified. Stream online status is not evidence that all songs match, nor are enrolled timestamps proof the active worker index is complete. No defensible current completeness or accuracy figure is established here. A missing detection is not evidence of no play.
- **Limits of reports and data:** broadcaster query is limited to 2,000 detections per day and duration is rounded to whole minutes, with a 180-second fallback if absent; this is not verified actual on-air duration (`lib/db/broadcaster.ts`, `lib/cue-sheet.ts`). The airplay feed defaults to 10,000 rows and caps at 50,000, without pagination; filtering or reconciliation is needed and a full export cannot be assumed (`app/api/feed/airplay/route.ts`). Evidence may be absent; clip access is controlled separately. Do not distribute private evidence paths as if they were public playable audio. Catalogue metadata and split/rights claims require verification.
- **Not built or not verified:** partner-specific URSB/UCC accounts, least-privilege institutional workspaces, consent/data-sharing controls, a CMS integration, automated legal compliance reporting, and validated TV/venue monitoring. Current approved CMO accounts have broad workspace access; **do not give a regulator, distributor or other partner a CMO role merely to demonstrate this proposal**. Agree a safe access model and test RLS first. Where necessary share minimised, reviewed samples under an agreement rather than opening a workspace.
- **Rollout gates:** secure permission from relevant station/rights holders and lawful basis for processing and sharing audio/personal data; legal review of licence and retention; decide roles, cross-border processing, deletion, incident response and contracts. Verify any required PDPO registration and reporting against [PDPO guidance](https://pdpo.go.ug/media/2022/01/20102021105143-Registration_Classification_and_Guidance_Notes.pdf) and counsel; no compliance certification is claimed. Confirm acceptance and technical requirements with each organisation. Payments remain on hold.

## Proposed 90-day pilot — subject to agreement and readiness

1. **Weeks 1–2, scope:** select a small number of willing stations and authorised recordings, nominate partner and East Sound owners, agree data permissions, benchmark logs, reporting period, retention, access, appeal process, budget and numerical acceptance thresholds. Review operational readiness and establish baseline. The schedule starts only when these prerequisites are met.
2. **Weeks 3–6, observe:** monitor permitted radio feeds; record uptime and outages, unmatched samples, index status and catalogue changes. Prepare reviewable weekly results; do not treat zero results as proof that no music aired.
3. **Weeks 7–10, reconcile:** independently compare against agreed ground-truth playout logs and, where permitted, listen to a representative adjudicated sample. Record missed, false, duplicate and disputed events, missing evidence and timestamps. Agree a process for station/rights-holder corrections.
4. **Weeks 11–13, review:** deliver aggregate results and limitations, discuss corrective action, data deletion/return and a joint go/no-go decision. A real distribution or regulator use is a separate decision, not a pilot outcome guaranteed in advance.

**Proposed measures; thresholds must be set before measurement:**
- Coverage: actual monitored time / agreed scheduled time per station, excluding no periods silently; report outages and permissions. This is stream availability, not percentage of all stations in Uganda.
- Recall on a defined enrolled-repertoire benchmark: matched eligible reference plays / all eligible reference plays; state sample size and exclusions. False-match precision: adjudicated correct detections / all adjudicated detections in the sampled set. Use independent ground truth and disclose selection bias.
- Duplicates and timestamp error: duplicate detections per eligible play; difference between reference and detected time, stating clock, time zone and tolerance. Keep missing and unmatched cases visible.
- Data quality: share of records with reviewed identifiers/credits and with accessible authorised evidence, each using all applicable detections as denominator. Measure unresolved disagreements and median time to resolve with censoring and sample size.
- Workflow: staff minutes per reviewed report and partner-accepted corrections. Do not equate an accepted report with royalties paid, legal compliance or audience reach.

## Corrections to the attached guide before external circulation

The PDF is an input, not an authoritative description of current readiness. Replace its absolute claims with qualified language:
- Pages 1–3, 6, 8–9, 11: “every song/play”, “every clip”, “continuously 30 seconds” and “royalties paid on what was actually played” are not established performance or operational guarantees. Say matches may be missed or incorrect; evidence and coverage vary; CMO decisions remain independent. Radio online does not verify end-to-end matching.
- Pages 3 and 5: do not promise every detection has a clip, is publicly playable or that a full-quality master never leaves a device without checking the deployed upload/storage path and permissions. Public sharing has legal and access implications.
- Pages 8 and 11–13: do not describe exports as UPRS-approved or ready to file without confirmation; logs may be incomplete and durations estimated. A music-use report is not a legal compliance certificate or complete station log. A 90-day pilot and partner integration are proposals.
- Page 9: the catalogue is not an official national register, and a dispute queue does not guarantee legal settlement or prevent a royalty payment elsewhere.
- Pages 10 and 14: venue devices and TV are future/experimental, not deployed capacity. Payment development and charging remain on hold; do not promise pricing or activation dates.
- Pages 11–12: validate the statistical, law and CMS claims with primary sources/current stakeholders before reusing them; do not assert East Sound is the promised mechanism, that the CMS needs East Sound, or that any organisation endorses it.
- Page 13: do not assert legal or data-protection compliance, that only clips are retained, or that every figure is traceable, until legal and operational review verifies the entire deployed pipeline. Establish rights, retention, processors, residency and privacy controls explicitly.
- Page 14: English/Luganda/Swahili translations need review; email, WhatsApp and SMS depend on configuration and actual delivery verification. Treat dated station counts as point-in-time, not live guarantees.

## Suggested next steps

1. Share the public `/partners` page and this brief with prospective partners; request a discovery meeting rather than claiming affiliation.
2. Confirm URSB/UPRS/UCC/CMS counterparts, current priorities, data format and minimum safeguards; assign a contact and record feedback.
